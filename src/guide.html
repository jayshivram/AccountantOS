Part I

ROE & Foreign Exchange Gain / Loss

How forex differences arise, what the ROE voucher does in Tally 9, and how to clear year-end forex balances for DTB Bank USD and Amana Bank USD.

Section 01

What is foreign exchange gain/loss?

Why the same amount of USD produces different TZS figures depending on when you transact.

When your business transacts in USD, every entry is recorded in TZS at the exchange rate on that specific day. Because the rate changes constantly, the TZS value of the same USD amount differs from one transaction to the next.

Over time, this creates TZS differences that do not represent actual cash — they are purely the result of rate movements. These are called foreign exchange differences, and accounting standards require you to recognise them at year-end.

Two types of forex difference

Type When it arises Example Status at year-end
Realized A transaction is fully COMPLETED — money received and paid at different rates Received USD at 2,600; paid out same USD at 2,500. The 100 TZS/USD difference is locked in. Already happened. Leave it in P&L. No year-end entry needed.
Unrealized You STILL HOLD foreign currency at year-end, recorded at an old rate You have $307.67 in DTB. It was recorded at blended rate 2,210 but today's rate is 2,450. The gap is unrealized. Must be recognised at year-end via ROE voucher.
Simple analogy
Imagine you have a jar of USD coins. Every time you put a coin in or took one out, you wrote down the TZS value at that day's rate. At year-end your jar has $307.67 in it, but the TZS total you wrote down for those coins doesn't match today's value of $307.67.

Unrealized gain/loss = you still have the coins in the jar. Revalue them to today's rate.
Realized gain/loss = you already spent the coins. The TZS difference is done.
Section 02

What the ROE voucher does in Tally 9

ROE = Rate of Exchange. A special voucher that revalues a foreign currency ledger without moving any actual cash.

Throughout the year, every USD transaction is posted at that day's rate. By 31 December, if you have a remaining USD balance, the TZS value in your ledger is a blended average of all the different rates used during the year — not today's market rate.

The ROE voucher says: "I know the current rate is X. Please update the TZS value of my USD balance to reflect today's rate." No cash moves. Only the TZS representation of the USD balance is adjusted.

What Tally does automatically

Tally's ROE calculation
Unrealized Forex Difference
= USD Closing Balance × (Current Rate − Blended Recorded Rate)

If result is positive → Forex GAIN (currency you hold is worth MORE in TZS)
If result is negative → Forex LOSS (currency you hold is worth LESS in TZS)
The offset always goes to: Gain/(Loss) on Exchange ledger
The ROE voucher only works if you have a remaining USD balance
If the actual USD balance is zero, then USD × any rate = TSH 0. There is nothing to revalue. The ROE voucher would produce no adjustment. This is why Amana Bank USD cannot be fixed with an ROE — its actual USD balance is $0. Only Tally ledgers with a non-zero foreign currency balance can use ROE.

Why DTB has an unrealized gain

DTB still holds $307.67. Those dollars were recorded at an average (blended) rate that is lower than today's rate of 2,450 TSH/USD. So the TZS value sitting in the ledger (680,479.50) is lower than what those dollars are actually worth today (753,791.50). The gap — TSH 73,312 — is money your books are understating. It is a gain.

Why Amana has a rate residual instead

Every single dollar that entered the Amana Bank USD account also left it — the account reached exactly $0. But because deposits came in at different TZS rates than the payments went out, the TZS flows did not perfectly cancel. Some deposits were at higher rates (e.g. 2,680), some payments were at lower rates (e.g. 2,450), so the TZS total received exceeded the TZS total paid by 41,370.04. This is a realized, accumulated rate difference sitting as a Cr balance in the ledger.

The rates used across 2025 on Amana Bank USD
Transactions were recorded at rates ranging from 2,485 to 2,680 TSH/USD throughout the year. When you receive $1,000 at 2,680 (TSH 2,680,000) and later pay $1,000 at 2,500 (TSH 2,500,000), you have TSH 180,000 more on the credit side than the debit side — even though USD netted to zero. Multiply this effect across hundreds of transactions and you get the 41,370.04 residual.

Section 05

DTB Bank USD — ROE Voucher + Journal

Two vouchers are required. The ROE voucher revalues the USD balance at the year-end rate. The Journal then clears the resulting Gain/(Loss) on Exchange balance.

Complete these in order — ROE first, then Journal
The ROE voucher creates the forex adjustment entry automatically. Once saved, the Gain/(Loss) on Exchange ledger will carry a balance of TSH 73,312.00 Cr. The Journal entry in Step 3 then clears that balance. Posting the Journal before the ROE voucher will result in debiting a zero-balance account.

Step 1 — Verify the ROE Voucher Type exists in Tally

Before posting, confirm that an ROE voucher type has been configured in this company. If it has been used before, skip to Step 2. If this is the first time, follow the setup below.

How to create the ROE Voucher Type (one-time setup)
1. Go to Gateway of Tally → Accounts Info → Voucher Types → Create

2. Fill in the following fields:
   • Name: ROE
   • Type of Voucher: Journal
   • Abbreviation: ROE
   • Use for forex revaluation: Yes
   • Default Voucher Class: Leave blank or set to ROE

3. Save. The ROE voucher type will now appear in the Accounting Vouchers screen and can be accessed via F10: Rev Jml.

Note: If F10: Rev Jml does not appear in Accounting Vouchers, go to F11: Features → Accounting Features → set Allow multi-currency to Yes. Save and re-enter Accounting Vouchers.

Step 2 — Post the ROE Voucher

Go to Gateway of Tally → Accounting Vouchers
Press F10: Rev Jml. Tally will prompt for a voucher type — select ROE. The Voucher Class field at the top should display ROE.
Set the date to 31-Dec-2025
In the Account field, select: Gain/(Loss) on Exchange
In Particulars, select: DTB BANK USD. Tally will prompt for the new exchange rate — enter 2,450
Tally automatically calculates the adjustment amount. Verify it shows TSH 73,312.00 — this should match the Forex Gain/Loss report from Section 4. Save the voucher.

Result after ROE: DTB BANK USD ledger is now stated at TSH 753,791.50 Dr — the correct value at the year-end rate of 2,450. Gain/(Loss) on Exchange shows TSH 73,312.00 Cr.

Step 3 — Journal Entry to clear Gain/(Loss) on Exchange

The ROE voucher leaves a balance in the Gain/(Loss) on Exchange ledger. A Journal entry is required to transfer this balance to Cash. This is consistent with the treatment applied in the prior year (December 2024 Journal No. 2).

Journal — Transfer DTB Forex Gain to Cash
Voucher Type: Journal (F7)  ·  Date: 31-Dec-2025
AccountDr (TSH)Cr (TSH)
Dr  Gain/(Loss) on Exchange73,312.00—
Cr  Cash—73,312.00
This entry zeros the Gain/(Loss) on Exchange ledger and recognises the forex gain as a credit to the Cash account — consistent with the December 2024 year-end treatment where Journal No. 2 carried the same structure: Dr Gain/(Loss) on Exchange / Cr Cash for TSH 5,703,512.78.

Result after Journal: Gain/(Loss) on Exchange balance = TSH 0.00. DTB BANK USD is correctly stated at the 31 December 2025 rate. Cash ledger reflects the recognised forex gain.

Section 06

Amana Bank USD — Journal Entry + Optional Cash Offset

No ROE voucher is needed. The process involves one required journal to clear the bank ledger, followed by an optional second journal to transfer the gain to Cash — mirroring the DTB treatment.

Why no ROE voucher for Amana?
The ROE voucher revalues a USD balance at a new rate. The Amana Bank USD account has $0 USD remaining — there is nothing to revalue. $0 × any rate = TSH 0. The Cr balance of TSH 41,370.04 is a residual TZS difference from transactions completed at varying rates throughout the year, not from a remaining USD position.

Section 07

Why the entries go in that direction

A Cr balance on a ledger is zeroed by adding a Dr. Here is the logic.

Understanding Cr and Dr balances on a bank ledger

In Tally's bank ledger view:

Entry typeEffect on bank ledgerReal-world meaning
Credit (Cr)Increases the Cr sideMoney received / deposited into the account
Debit (Dr)Increases the Dr sideMoney paid out / withdrawn from the account
Cr closing balanceCredits > DebitsThe account has a positive TZS balance remaining
Dr closing balanceDebits > CreditsMore went out than came in (overdraft in standard terms)

To zero a Cr balance — you must add a Debit

Common mistake — wrong direction
If the ledger shows Cr 41,370.04 and you post a Credit entry of 41,370.04, the balance becomes Cr 82,740.08 — it doubles.

Cr + Cr = bigger Cr balance. That is the wrong direction.
Correct — add a Debit to cancel a Cr balance
Cr 41,370.04 (existing) + Dr 41,370.04 (new entry) = TSH 0

Debit the bank ledger → reduces the Cr balance → brings it to zero. ✓

Why this is not "paying out" money

Debiting a bank account normally means cash leaving. But here, no cash is actually moving — the USD balance is already zero at the actual bank. This Dr entry is purely a book adjustment to reconcile the TZS residual left by different exchange rates. The offset (Cr Gain/Loss on Exchange) records that this TZS difference was a forex gain, not a real receipt or payment.

One-line summary of the direction logic

  • Cr balance on ledger → needs a Dr entry to zero it → Dr Bank / Cr Gain/Loss on Exchange
  • Dr balance on ledger → needs a Cr entry to zero it → Cr Bank / Dr Gain/Loss on Exchange
  • Adding more Cr to a Cr balance → always makes it worse → never do this to zero a Cr balance
Section 08

Quick-reference terminology

All key terms in one place.

ROE Voucher
Rate of Exchange voucher. A special Tally 9 voucher that revalues a foreign currency ledger at a new exchange rate. Does not move cash — only adjusts TZS representation of the USD balance. Only works when USD balance ≠ 0.
Unrealized Gain/Loss
Forex difference on balances you still hold. Arises when the current exchange rate differs from the rate at which existing USD holdings were originally recorded. Must be recognised at year-end via ROE.
Realized Gain/Loss
Forex difference on completed transactions. Arises when you received and paid USD at different rates and the transaction is done. Already in the books — no adjustment needed.
Rate Residual
TZS difference left after all USD flows cancel to zero. The Amana Bank situation: USD = $0 but TZS ≠ 0 because deposits came in at higher rates than payments went out. Cleared via journal entry.
Gain/(Loss) on Exchange
The Tally ledger that captures all forex adjustments. It is the automatic offset in ROE vouchers and the other side in forex journal entries. Should be zeroed out by year-end through a journal transfer to P&L or the appropriate account.
Blended Rate
The weighted average TZS/USD rate across all transactions. Tally uses this internally to calculate the "Closing Balance at Transacted Rates" in the forex report. It is never a single rate — it is the net result of all transactions posted at different rates.
Cr Balance on Bank
Credits > Debits in the bank ledger. Means more TZS was received (via deposits/receipts) than paid out. To bring to zero: add a Debit entry. Adding a Credit would increase it further.
Dr Balance on Bank
Debits > Credits in the bank ledger. Means more TZS was paid out than received — unusual for a normal bank account. To bring to zero: add a Credit entry.

Withholding Tax on Professional Fees

When paying for specialised services, the payer must deduct tax before paying the professional.

Withholding tax on professional fees means that when a person or company pays for professional services (accounting, auditing, legal services, consulting, etc.), they must deduct a certain percentage of tax before paying the professional and remit that tax to the government. In Tanzania this tax is administered by the Tanzania Revenue Authority.

1 · What are Professional Fees?

Professional fees are payments made for specialised services provided by professionals. Examples include fees paid to:

  • Accountants
  • Auditors
  • Lawyers
  • Consultants
  • Engineers
  • Architects

2 · How Withholding Works

The client deducts withholding tax from the total fee.
The client pays the remaining amount to the accountant.
The deducted tax is sent to the government through the Tanzania Revenue Authority.

3 · Example (Accountancy Service)

Suppose:
  • An accountant charges 2,000,000 TZS for audit services.
  • Withholding tax rate = 5% (resident professional services).

Step 1: Calculate withholding tax

5% × 2,000,000 = 100,000 TZS

Step 2: Payment

ItemAmount (TZS)
Total professional fee2,000,000
Less: withholding tax (5%)100,000
Amount paid to accountant1,900,000

4 · Roles

Tax Credit — Not an Extra Tax For the accountant, the withholding tax is not an extra tax. It is treated as a tax credit when they file their annual income tax return with the Tanzania Revenue Authority.

Profit Ratios & Formulas

Gross profit margin, cost of sales ratio, and markup percentage explained.

Gross Profit / Revenue × 100

This formula calculates the Gross Profit Margin (percentage) — how much profit a business keeps from sales after covering the direct cost of producing goods or services.

  • Gross Profit = Revenue − Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)
  • Revenue = Total sales before expenses
Example
  • Revenue = 10,000 TZS
  • Cost of Goods Sold = 6,000 TZS
  • Gross Profit = 4,000 TZS
4,000 ÷ 10,000 × 100Gross Profit Margin = 40%

This means the business keeps 40% of its sales as gross profit before operating expenses.

Cost of Sales / Revenue × 100

This formula calculates the Cost of Sales Ratio — how much of the revenue is spent on producing or buying the products sold.

  • Cost of Sales (COGS) = Direct costs used to produce or purchase the goods/services sold.
  • Revenue = Total sales generated.
6,000 ÷ 10,000 × 100Cost of Sales Ratio = 60%
Note If Cost of Sales = 60%, then Gross Profit Margin = 40%, since:
Gross Profit Margin + Cost of Sales Ratio = 100%

Gross Profit / Cost of Sales × 100

This formula calculates the Markup Percentage — how much profit is added on top of the cost of a product.

4,000 ÷ 6,000 × 100Markup = 66.67%
Important difference
Markup Percentage = Profit based on cost
Gross Profit Margin = Profit based on revenue

From the example: Markup = 66.67%  ·  Gross Profit Margin = 40%
FormulaBased OnResult
Gross Profit MarginRevenue40%

Prior Year Adjustments

Amounts disallowed by TRA after a tax audit and their effect on Assets, Liabilities, and Capital.

Prior year adjustments are amounts disallowed by the Tanzania Revenue Authority after a tax audit. These adjustments correct errors or overstatements made in previous financial periods.

The accounting equation is: Assets = Capital + Liabilities

A) Example: Disallowed Creditors

Scenario

The Tanzania Revenue Authority disallows TZS 50,000,000 of creditors. The business recorded payables that are not valid, so Liabilities (Creditors) decrease.

1
What changes? Creditors (Liabilities) ↓ by 50,000,000
2
Apply accounting equation — Assets = Capital + Liabilities. If liabilities decrease and assets stay the same → Capital must increase
3
Why capital increases — The business previously recorded an expense now rejected → expenses were overstated → profit was actually higher → retained earnings (capital) increases
LiabilitiesCreditors ↓ (reduced)
CapitalEquity ↑ (increased)
AssetsNo change
ReasonExpenses were overstated

B) Example: Disallowed Debtors

Scenario

The Tanzania Revenue Authority disallows TZS 50,000,000 of debtors. The business recorded receivables that are not valid, so Assets (Debtors) decrease.

1
What changes? Debtors (Assets) ↓ by 50,000,000
2

Section 15

Tax Credit Written Off due to TRA Assessment

When TRA disallows previously recognised tax recoverable balances.

Tax credit written off occurs when the Tanzania Revenue Authority disallows previously recognised tax recoverable balances. Tax recoverable is treated as an asset, representing amounts expected to be refunded.

Accounting Treatment

  • Opening balance — Represents tax credit brought forward
  • Tax credit written off — Occurs when TRA disallows prior claims: reduces assets (tax recoverable) and reduces capital (equity)
  • Tax recoverable for the year — New tax credit generated in the current year: increases assets and increases capital
  • Closing balance — Carried forward to the next year

Key Principle

Tax recoverable= Asset
Write-offAsset ↓ → Capital ↓
New tax creditAsset ↑ → Capital ↑
NatureAlways affects both Asset and Capital equally

Flow Formula

Closing balance = Opening balance − Write-off + Current year tax credit

Section 16

Closing Stock & Profit / Loss

How closing stock directly affects Cost of Goods Sold and the profit or loss figure.

Closing stock directly affects Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) and therefore impacts the profit or loss figure.

Effect on Profit

↑ Closing Stock increases → Profit ↑
↓ Closing Stock decreases → Profit ↓

Effect on Loss

↑ Closing Stock increases → Loss ↓
↓ Closing Stock decreases → Loss ↑
Remember:
Closing stock ↑ → COGS ↓ → Better results
Closing stock ↓ → COGS ↑ → Worse results

Why closing stock affects profit

COGS Formula
COGS = Opening Stock + Purchases − Closing Stock

Gross Profit = Revenue − COGS
Higher closing stock → Lower COGS → Higher Gross Profit
Practical implication
If at year-end you count more stock than your records show, COGS decreases and profit increases — which also means a higher tax liability. If stock is understated, COGS is overstated, profit is understated, and TRA may raise a prior year adjustment (see Section 14C).
